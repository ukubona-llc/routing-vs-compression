On binary form and the pentad mapping

Yes — binary (AB or A‖B:‖) is the minimal viable architecture of most lasting Western tonal music, and the mapping onto the pentad is sharp. Part A is the exploratory phrase: melodic contour rises, harmonic motion is more open, voice-leading has more stepwise "search" and chromatic inflection — the Unmapped / Explorers moment. Part B is the return, the closing gesture, the more formulaic cadence, where the Mapped / Pheromones take over: harmonic rhythm tightens, the bass becomes more functional, the cadence is prepared and delivered.

The reordered operators track the musical reality better than the original dual-process ordering for this case: Territory → Mapped/Pheromones (B, the established harmonic highway) → Colony (the threshold decision) → Unmapped/Explorers (A, the deliberate higher-entropy search) → Falsifiable/Eureka.

Resolution-by-reclassification

The analysis of Bars 1–2 in Stainer's Cross of Jesus hits on a fundamental system insight: resolution-by-reclassification. In standard voice-leading, a Isus(2,4) chord demands physical voice movement — the 4th steps down to the 3rd, the 2nd resolves to the tonic or 3rd. But Stainer bypasses upper-voice movement entirely. By moving only the bass step down (G→F♯), the static upper tones are instantly re-indexed as the 3rd, 5th, and 7th of a first-inversion V7 chord. The upper state variables didn't change position — the coordinate frame beneath them shifted.

This double payoff — where the arrival on I at Bar 2, Beat 1 simultaneously discharges the newly minted V7 and delivers the long-delayed 3rd (B) — is a masterclass in minimal-energy state transitions.

Stage III as phase transition

Reframing Stage III (Colony / Which) away from a centralized "router" module toward an emergent threshold aligns directly with physical swarm behavior and non-equilibrium systems. No central controller: in ant foraging, routing is an emergent property of local density. When pheromone concentration is low, variance dominates, sending scouts into high-entropy search space (Stage IV). The moment signal density crosses a critical threshold γ, exploration is suppressed and the system collapses into low-entropy execution (Stage II).

What computes the threshold?

The swarm analogy is excellent, but it leaves one question unresolved: what computes the threshold? In an ant colony, pheromone concentration is the threshold variable — physics does the routing. In tonal music, the "pheromone" isn't physical. It's expectation. That changes everything.

Stage III isn't routing. It's compression.

Stage III isn't asking "which way?" It is asking "can I explain this with my existing model?" If yes, don't explore. If no, wake up Stage IV. That is precisely what modern LLMs, Bayesian inference, predictive processing, and tonal listening all do.

Model updating, not routing

The astonishing thing about the Isus(2,4) observation is that nothing actually resolves. The notes remain — only the coordinate system changes, because the bass moved. The listener performs the reclassification: that is not routing, it is model updating. The brain has compressed the same sensory data under a cheaper explanation. The same phenomenon occurs in vision — a Necker cube flips; nothing on the page changes, only the interpretation.

Phase transition in description length
L_explore > L_compress

When the current observations can be encoded more cheaply by reclassifying than by exploring, the system collapses into exploitation. That is exactly what happens in the hymn: instead of moving four voices, the composer moves one bass note. The listener performs the expensive computation. Energy saved.

Where AI fails

Not because systems lack exploration, and not because they lack heuristics — but because they often commit to a compressed model too early. Once an LLM settles on an interpretation, every subsequent token reinforces that interpretation, much like a runaway pheromone trail: if the initial framing is slightly wrong, the model can become increasingly confident in the wrong trajectory because each prediction conditions the next.

In the hymn, Stainer delays that commitment just long enough. The suspension creates ambiguity; the bass motion resolves it with minimal effort. The system preserves optionality until there is enough evidence for reinterpretation. Design principle for AI systems: too low a threshold → premature convergence, confirmation bias, local minima. Too high a threshold → endless exploration, paralysis, excessive computation. The sweet spot: maintain ambiguity until one reinterpretation yields a substantially cheaper, more predictive description of the observed data.

On Stage III: routing vs. compression

These aren't competing framings, and I'd push back gently on treating OpenAI's version as a refinement that supersedes the swarm reading. Pheromone concentration and description-length reduction are the same threshold-crossing event described at two different levels — one is the implementation substrate, the other is the computation being implemented. This isn't new to the pentad: it's the standard exploration/exploitation trade-off, where "concentration" is a running proxy for posterior confidence. OpenAI's L_explore > L_compress is a cleaner formalization, not a correction. Worth keeping both, folded into Stage III together, rather than letting it read as OpenAI beating xAI and Gemini.
